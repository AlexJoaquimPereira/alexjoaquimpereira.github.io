When I pick up a new programming language, tutorials only get me so far.
What actually makes things stick is picking one tiny idea and building it end
to end — something small enough to finish in a weekend, but real enough that
it has inputs, logic, and output.

## Keep the scope small

The project has to be boringly small. A script that renames files, a tiny
quiz app, a CLI that fetches something from an API. If the idea needs a
database or a design system, it's too big for step one.

## Let the problems teach the syntax

Instead of memorising syntax first, I look things up as the project demands
them. The second or third time I need the same construct, it's mine. By the
end, the project itself becomes the notes.

> Build something small, finish it completely, then build the next one. The
> compounding is the point.

## What's next

Right now I'm applying this to Python and JavaScript — the two languages I'm
actively exploring — and keeping the finished pieces small enough to actually
share here.
